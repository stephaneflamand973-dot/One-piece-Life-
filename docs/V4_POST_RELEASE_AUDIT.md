## Next major evolution candidate: LIFE JOURNEY ENGINE

Do **not** add more dashboards, gauges, currencies or routine buttons.

The next major evolution should make the player's personal trajectory as alive as the world already is.

### 1. Natural mobility
Career, missions, organizations, relationships and world events should naturally create transfers, deployments, invitations, relocation opportunities, temporary assignments and expedition offers.

The player should not remain on one island for twenty years unless their choices genuinely lead there. Travel should still matter, but the game should surface meaningful reasons to move instead of requiring the player to browse routes manually.

### 2. Emergent relationships
Relationships already evolve autonomously, but major life transitions are still mostly menu actions.

The engine should surface interesting decisions when conditions naturally appear: a friendship becoming romantic, a partner proposing a more serious commitment, separation caused by incompatible trajectories, family versus ambition, a child becoming relevant to the player's future, a mentor becoming a peer, a friend joining an organization, or a loved one being affected by war, faction change or relocation.

The player decides the important moment. The engine creates the opportunity.

### 3. Career arcs instead of pure rank ladders
Promotion thresholds work, but similar careers converge too predictably.

Future career progression should include assignments, patrons and rivals, promotion opportunities, failures and stalled periods, faction-specific career events, transfers and command opportunities, and specialization-specific accomplishments.

Ranks remain readable. Complexity stays behind the scenes.

### 4. Personal-world causality
World history and personal history should increasingly intersect. A war can relocate the player's unit or family; an affiliated crew can create a personal enemy; a canon divergence can change a career opportunity; a relationship with a canonical character can open or close an arc; a promotion can change which sagas the player can materially influence.

### 5. Legacy that begins before death
Dynasty currently becomes most visible when the player dies.

The next system should let legacy develop during life: children grow, apprentices inherit techniques or reputation, protégés form their own careers, family or crew members remember major choices, and succession becomes a long-running possibility rather than only a death-screen mechanic.

### 6. Keep the BitLife philosophy
Target experience:
- **AVANCER stays the central action**
- important choices appear automatically
- no daily micromanagement
- no compulsory relationship grinding
- no compulsory travel grinding
- no extra permanent screen unless absolutely necessary
- keep the current five main tabs
- continue using compact reports and the existing Attention system

## Decision

V4.0 solved the largest world-simulation weaknesses without sacrificing flow. The next large improvement should therefore focus on **the life journey of the player**, not on adding another layer of world simulation.

The working concept is **LIFE JOURNEY ENGINE**. The final version number should only be chosen when its implementation scope is locked.
